Impact of Microplastics on Marine Fauna and Pelagic Ecosystems

Executive Synthesis

Microplastic particulates (<5 mm) have permeated every strata of the marine pelagic environment. Beyond passive ingestion, these non-biodegradable polymeric matrices function as vectors for persistent organic pollutants (POPs) while disrupting cellular biomechanics, gamete motility, and structural calcification across keystone marine taxa.

1. Trophic Bioaccumulation & Physical Ingestion

Pelagic filter-feeders and benthic detritivores ingest microplastics indiscriminately due to morphological overlaps with native zooplankton. Ingestion induces severe physical abrasions along epithelial tracts and causes pseudo-satiation that diminishes total caloric assimilation Verified Source marinedebris.noaa.gov NOAA Marine Debris Program — Benthic Studies "Documented high-frequency microfragment penetration in intestinal villi..." Cited 3 times Open source ↗ . In apical teleosts and chondrichthyes, translocated secondary nanoplastics cross cell membranes, instigating chronic lipid peroxidation and compromised reproductive fecundity .

2. Chemical Toxicity & Additive Leaching

Synthesized polymers are not chemically inert; they act as passive sponges for ambient hydrophobic contaminants, aggregating polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) at concentrations exceeding ambient seawater by orders of magnitude . Upon ingestion, the thermal and enzymatic gradients within digestive tracts accelerate chemical desorptive kinetics, uncoupling endocrine-disrupting phthalates into circulating hemolymph .

3. Impacts on Coral Reef Morphogenesis

Hermatypic corals directly ingest buoyant polyethylene micro-beads, exhausting metabolic reserves via active clearing cycles while impairing endosymbiotic dinoflagellate photosystem II efficiency .

While observational field reports show acute bleaching vulnerability, lab mesocosm trials indicate scleractinian recovery once mechanical particulate sediment is removed within 72 hours.
